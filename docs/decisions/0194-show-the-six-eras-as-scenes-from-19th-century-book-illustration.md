# 0194. Show the six eras as scenes from 19th-century book illustration

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-06

## Context and problem statement

The owner wants six tiered exhibits of the display case, one per era, for the artworks the learner adds from that era, each with an object or scene symbolic of the era (idea ATL-513, [ADR-0160](0160-gamify-learning-as-a-museum-career.md)). The look and the texts are designed first; the counting is built later in its own issue. What do the six count, what do they show, how are they named and what do their curiosities tell?

## Decision drivers

- Engravings come from a public-domain, CC0 or CC BY source ([ADR-0160](0160-gamify-learning-as-a-museum-career.md)) and are named after what they show ([ADR-0185](0185-name-each-exhibit-after-what-its-engraving-shows.md)).
- Six exhibits shown side by side should read as one set.
- No object repeats one already in the display case (temple, globe, telescope, orloj, camera obscura, hourglass).
- Each tier reveals a curiosity with one source that states every fact (`voice-and-tone.md`).

## Considered options

For what is counted: artworks added to the era; artworks of the era settled in memory.

For the tiers: 1, 10, 25, 50, 100; 1, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250.

For the look: a single object on generated wall hatching for every era; scenes for every era; a mix of prints from the eras themselves (Delsenbach's Holy Lance, the Nova Reperta astrolabe, Hamilton's vases, Amman's printer); 19th-century book illustration for every era.

For the objects, era by era: a hand axe or a torch; writing or an amphora; the Holy Lance of Saint Maurice, a sword or a shield with a coat of arms; an astrolabe or a printing press; a steam locomotive or a camera; a vision of the future (Verne's projectile, Robida's téléphonoscope, flying machines, Villemard's "En l'an 2000").

For the curiosities: how artworks of the era were made; stories about the engraving's motif; the first tier on the motif and the rest on making.

## Decision outcome

Chosen by the owner in ATL-522.

### Counting

- Each exhibit counts the learner's artworks of its era, as "Kolekcja" counts all of them; an artwork without an era counts in none. Settled artworks stay with "Pamięć".
- Six tiers: 1, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250. The owner's collection after three weeks had 91 modern and 90 early modern artworks, so 100 would have closed the two most collected eras within a month; 250 stays a goal there and a long one for prehistory. The same thresholds for every era keep them easy to read.
- Exhibit codes are the era codes: `prehistory`, `antiquity`, `middle_ages`, `early_modern`, `modern`, `contemporary`.

### Look

- Every exhibit shows a scene with people from a 19th-century illustrated book, in wood engraving. Objects on generated wall and prints from the eras themselves were rendered first and dropped: the techniques, framings and backgrounds read as six unrelated pictures. Scenes were preferred to objects for all six.
- The Holy Lance and the astrolabe, the owner's first choices, were not found in 19th-century book illustration.
- Wide crops for all six. Variants in `tools/exhibit-art/manifest.json`:

- "Turniej w Chauvency" is an exception to [ADR-0185](0185-name-each-exhibit-after-what-its-engraving-shows.md), chosen by the owner: the engraving is an allegory (the knight "Auctor" on the horse "Querer" fights "Desconcierto", both with swords inscribed "Locura") standing for tournaments in general, and the name is a real one, the six-day tournament of October 1285 described by Jacques Bretel. The story of the object says the scene is allegorical. Perpignan, where Zawisza Czarny unhorsed John of Aragon, was dropped because it is remembered for one joust rather than a tournament.
- "Armarium Rzymianina" avoids a second library next to "Biblioteka w Lejdzie"; "Parowóz Pacific Railroad" names the railroad of the engraving rather than an inventor who did not build it.

### Curiosities

All six share one theme: how the artworks of the era were made, through materials, techniques and workshops. The full set of 36 is written now. Topics already in the display case were avoided, and topics about painting were kept to about half after the owner asked for less painting. "Księga z Kells" writes its numbers in words, an exception to `voice-and-tone.md` chosen by the owner.

### Consequences

- Good: the six read as one set in one technique, and the 19th-century view of every era is itself a story.
- Good: the curiosities teach how art was made in each era, across painting, sculpture, prints, textiles, ceramics and installations.
- Bad: one name departs from the rule of [ADR-0185](0185-name-each-exhibit-after-what-its-engraving-shows.md), and the locomotive and the knights are smaller or rougher than the other scenes.
- Bad: several curiosities run past the guide of about 350 characters, accepted by the owner, and many sources are museum blogs or the Archaeological Institute of America's magazine because Britannica, Smarthistory and several museums refused automated fetching.
